Why CAs and Lawyers Need a Professional Website (Not Just a Visiting Card)

Prospects Google a CA or lawyer before ever meeting them. A services page, credentials, and a confidentiality-conscious inquiry option is what separates a credible practice from a visiting card with a phone number.

AB Labs6 min readPublished July 26, 2026
CA & LawyersProfessional ServicesCredibility

Before a client ever calls a CA or a lawyer, they almost always Google them first — to check if the practice looks established, what services are actually offered, and whether anyone else has worked with them before. A LinkedIn profile helps, but it's a shared platform designed for networking, not a dedicated space for your practice, your services, and your credibility.

💡

Quick answer: A CA or lawyer's website needs a clear services page, visible credentials and experience, and a professional, confidentiality-conscious way to inquire — a LinkedIn profile alone doesn't cover any of these well.

Isn't LinkedIn enough?

LinkedIn is useful for professional networking, but it's not built to represent a practice — you can't fully control layout, you're competing for attention against everyone else's posts, and you can't easily add a services breakdown, a proper inquiry form, or content specific to your practice area. A website gives you a space entirely dedicated to establishing that credibility, on your own terms.

What the homepage should include

  • Services list: specific practice areas (tax filing, GST compliance, corporate law, family law, etc.), not a vague "legal services" label.
  • Credentials and experience: qualifications, years in practice, bar/institute registration where relevant.
  • Client testimonials: with permission, ideally naming the type of matter handled rather than specifics.
  • A professional inquiry option: not a public form that broadcasts sensitive details, but a clear, private way to start a conversation.

"A prospect who can't tell what you actually specialize in from your homepage will simply move to the next name on their search results."

Should fees be mentioned on the site?

Full public pricingRealistic middle ground
Rarely appropriate — most CA/legal fees are matter-specificState that fees are discussed after understanding the matter, or give a starting range for common services (like GST filing)

Complete silence on cost, though, often reads as evasive. A short, honest note on how pricing works — even without exact numbers — removes unnecessary hesitation.

How blogging helps a CA or lawyer's website rank

A blog answering common questions in plain language (like "What documents do I need for GST registration?" or "When should I consult a lawyer for a property dispute?") does two things at once: it demonstrates expertise to a visitor, and it gives Google specific, searchable content to rank — the same fundamentals in our website SEO checklist apply directly here.

What contact method works best for sensitive inquiries

Unlike a salon or restaurant, legal and financial matters are often sensitive, so a public WhatsApp button with a generic pre-filled message isn't always the right fit. A short private inquiry form, combined with a WhatsApp or call option clearly marked for general questions, strikes a better balance — see our WhatsApp lead conversion guide for the underlying principles, adapted here for confidentiality.

A short credibility checklist

  1. Are your specific practice areas listed, not just a generic label?
  2. Are your credentials and experience visible on the homepage?
  3. Is there at least a general sense of how fees work?
  4. Is there a professional, private way to start an inquiry?
  5. Does the site include any content (blog/FAQ) that demonstrates expertise?
